CUSTOMER STORY / IKARAO

Six people. $20M. No analyst.

Ikarao’s operations team spent 60–70% of its time reading data. Before the real work could begin, someone had to move between Seller Central, LingXing ERP, keyword tools, and spreadsheets. The team wanted answers—not another reporting ritual.

At roughly $20 million in annual Amazon US revenue, Ikarao had reached a scale where every small movement mattered—but the company had only six people in operations and no dedicated data analyst.

Four marketplace operators covered the US, Europe, Canada, Mexico, and Walmart. Two more people handled affiliate marketing and PR. Together they managed about ten active ASINs, an average order value near $300, and more than $100,000 in monthly advertising spend. The catalog was compact; the number of decisions hidden inside it was not.

01WHAT SIX PEOPLE WERE CARRYING

Ten ASINs. Five daily workstreams. Every day.

A catalog of roughly ten ASINs can look manageable from a distance. At Ikarao’s volume, it created a dense daily analysis load. Under normal conditions, the team spent two to three hours reading reports before acting. When an issue surfaced—which happened often—the investigation could add another one or two hours.

The work began with a topline scan across every active ASIN, then moved into session-level diagnosis whenever sales changed. A traffic problem required one path; a conversion problem required another. Advertising demanded its own investigation into budget burn, CPC movement, competitor promotions, and whether spend was supporting real product velocity.

Organic rank and listing health formed two more streams. Hourly keyword volatility could signal a problem before it appeared in the daily average. Fulfillment lead time, suppressed listings, suspicious competitive activity, new negative reviews, inventory, inbound shipments, customs, and replenishment all competed for the same six people’s attention.

02HOW THEY USE PULSE

Ask. Don’t pull.

The operating change began by connecting Amazon Seller Central and LingXing ERP into one view. Seller Central contributed orders, revenue, ASIN-level velocity, customer mix, advertising performance, and session signals. LingXing contributed inventory, costs, fulfillment tracking, and inbound-shipment status. The team no longer had to rebuild that join for each question.

The interface to that connected context was ordinary language. Instead of choosing a report, exporting rows, cleaning columns, and building a ranking in a spreadsheet, an operator could ask which ASINs were growing fastest week over week. The answer arrived as a ranked list, with the strongest raw-volume and percentage changes separated so the team could read them correctly.

A second question changed how the team looked at advertising: which products could sell organically, and which depended almost entirely on paid traffic? That is a different lens from ROAS alone. It helps distinguish an ASIN whose position deserves protection from one whose revenue could disappear when spend pauses.

03THE WEEKLY CADENCE

Ask once. It runs every week.

The highest-leverage questions were not one-off investigations. They came back every Monday, every morning, or whenever spend moved outside expectations. Ikarao turned those repeated prompts into standing scheduled tasks so the analysis could be prepared before an operator opened a report.

The weekly business summary brought together the biggest wins, items needing attention, and signals to watch in the coming week. A daily stock-risk watch compared sales velocity with inventory levels. A weekly ad-spend review ranked where budget deserved more attention and where it might be reduced, using ROAS together with velocity rather than treating either metric in isolation.

Scheduling did not eliminate ad hoc analysis. Operators could still ask why a specific ASIN’s sessions dropped yesterday or whether a growth spike came from traffic, conversion, or paid demand. The difference was that recurring work no longer crowded out the unexpected work that needed human attention.

04THE OPERATING LEVERAGE

The analyst Ikarao was never going to hire

For Ikarao, the main result was not a prettier dashboard. It was time handed back to a small team. The customer story reports roughly 15 hours of analysis returned each week—about 750 hours over a working year. Questions that had required manual report pulling and spreadsheet work could come back as structured answers in under five minutes.

That cadence would ordinarily create pressure to add a dedicated analyst. Ikarao estimates the avoided role at about $120,000 a year and reports that no new headcount was added. The important qualifier is that this is Ikarao’s own operating context and hiring comparison, not a guaranteed saving for every team.

The six people still owned the decisions. They reviewed the evidence, interpreted promotions and competitive events, and chose what to do next. Pulse changed the cost of reaching that decision: less time assembling the evidence, more time applying the team’s market knowledge to it.

The connected view mattered because it changed the first step

Ikarao did not need another destination for charts. It needed a way for six people to begin with the business question instead of the mechanics of finding, exporting, and joining data. Connecting Seller Central and LingXing made the underlying context reusable; plain-language questions made that context accessible in the moment of work.

The result was a different operating rhythm: recurring questions prepared on schedule, unusual questions investigated as they appeared, and human judgment focused where the data alone could not decide. For a small team carrying a large Amazon business, that rhythm was the capacity gain.

About the Ikarao customer story

Data scope, reported outcomes, and automation boundaries.

What data did Ikarao connect?

The customer story describes Amazon Seller Central and LingXing ERP as connected. Seller Central supplied commercial and advertising signals; LingXing supplied inventory, costs, fulfillment, and inbound-shipment context. Coverage depends on authorized accounts and available history.

Does Pulse automatically change Ikarao’s ads or listings?

No. The workflows described here prepare analysis, monitoring, and recurring briefs for human review. They do not imply automatic changes to campaigns, bids, listings, inventory, orders, or payments.

Are the time and hiring savings guaranteed?

No. The figures are specific to Ikarao’s reported workload, team structure, and hiring comparison. They are not a benchmark or a promise of results for another company.

Was the $120,000 analyst cost an actual cash saving?

It is presented as an avoided annual hire, not a reduction to an existing payroll line. Ikarao reports that the workload would otherwise have required a dedicated analyst and that no new headcount was added.

Can the scheduled tasks make final operating decisions?

No. Scheduled tasks prepare recurring analysis and surface exceptions. Ikarao’s operators still review the evidence, apply business context, and decide what action to take.
